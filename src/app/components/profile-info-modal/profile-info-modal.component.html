Pick the level that best matches your overall daily life — not just your gym sessions. Your job, commute, and daily movement matter more than a few hours of exercise. We use this to size calorie adjustments — less active means smaller tweaks have a bigger effect, more active means you can handle (and may need) larger shifts.

Sedentary

Under 3,000 steps/day · No deliberate exercise

Desk job or remote worker. Majority of the day spent seated with minimal walking.

Lightly Active

5,000–7,500 steps/day · Light exercise 1–3 days/week

Standing or walking job (teacher, cashier, retail) — or a desk worker who adds light movement like daily walks.

Moderately Active

7,500–10,000 steps/day · Moderate exercise 3–5 days/week

On your feet most of the day (nurse, server, tradesperson) — or a desk worker with frequent intense workouts.

Very Active

10,000–12,500+ steps/day · Hard exercise 6–7 days/week

Physically demanding job (construction, agricultural worker) or an intense athletic training schedule.

Extra Active

Heavy daily labor + intense training

Heavy manual labor (logger, professional athlete, military training) combined with daily endurance or strength work.

How long you've been training consistently determines how fast you can gain muscle and how aggressively you can diet without losing it. We use this to set your safe rate-of-change ceiling and the size of calorie adjustments we suggest.

Beginner

Less than 1 year of consistent resistance training

New to lifting or returning after a long break. You can build muscle quickly — even in a mild deficit. A moderate surplus is enough during a bulk; no need to overeat.

Gentler loss ceiling (~0.7% BW/week) and smaller calorie adjustments to protect new muscle.

Intermediate

1–3 years of consistent resistance training

You've built a solid base and know your way around the gym. Muscle gain has slowed but is still meaningful. Standard cut/bulk approaches work well.

Standard thresholds: 0.5–1.0% BW/week loss range, ~200 kcal adjustment steps.

Advanced

3+ years of consistent resistance training

Muscle gain is slow (1–2 kg/year). Every gram is hard-earned, so surpluses stay tight and cuts are controlled. You can handle bigger dietary swings without losing muscle if protein is high.

Tighter gain ceiling (~0.35% BW/week), larger deficit steps (~250 kcal) when progress stalls.

Your natural build affects how your body responds to surplus and deficit. Pick the one that best describes you before training changed your shape. We use this to adjust surplus sizes, loss rate limits, and flag when body composition monitoring is especially important.

Ectomorph

Narrow shoulders, long limbs, naturally lean

You've always been thin and find it hard to gain weight. You may eat a lot without putting on mass. Think: distance-runner build.

Larger surplus recommended (~300 kcal). Calorie-dense foods (nuts, oils, shakes) help hit targets.

Mesomorph

Broad shoulders, athletic frame, gains muscle easily

You respond well to training and can shift body composition without extreme measures. Think: naturally athletic build.

Standard adjustments apply. You have flexibility in how aggressively you cut or bulk.

Endomorph

Wider hips, stockier frame, gains weight easily

You put on size quickly — both muscle and fat. Surplus phases need careful monitoring (watch waist, not just scale). Think: naturally solid/stocky build.

Tighter surplus, lower max loss rate (~0.8% BW/week), and body composition awareness in all advice.

Most people are a blend of types. Pick the closest match — your habits and training matter far more than any label.
