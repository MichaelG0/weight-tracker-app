BMI is a quick screen based only on your height and weight. It cannot tell muscle from fat, so it can misread people who carry a lot of muscle.

Why it can mislead

Muscle is denser than fat. A muscular or well-trained person can weigh more than the BMI categories expect while carrying a low body-fat percentage. Such a person may be labelled "Overweight" or even "Obese" despite being lean.

Where it is most useful

For people with little muscle mass, weight mostly reflects body fat, so BMI is a reasonable rough guide. It is also useful for spotting trends over time, since the same measure is applied each day.

Better measures for trained people

  • Waist-to-height ratio: keep your waist under half your height.
  • Body-fat percentage: from a DEXA scan, skinfold calipers, or bioimpedance (estimates vary by device).
  • Waist circumference and regular progress photos or measurements.
  • Strength and performance over time, which show whether you are gaining muscle or fat.

Treat this card as one rough signal, not a diagnosis. For health concerns, talk to a doctor or other qualified professional.
